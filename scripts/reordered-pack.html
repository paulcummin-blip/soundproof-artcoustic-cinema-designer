03Method

What makes great cinema sound?

A great cinema should place sounds where they belong, preserve the contrast between quiet detail and powerful moments, and maintain a consistent tonal character throughout the room.

Our approach is to balance these three qualities around the room, the seating and the client's priorities.

Spatial Resolution

Sound should come from where the filmmaker intended.

With a single loudspeaker, every sound comes from the same place. Stereo allows sounds to move from left to right. Surround sound adds movement beside and behind you, while immersive systems introduce height, allowing sounds to travel overhead.

The number of loudspeakers creates opportunities, but their positions are equally important.

The aim is for sounds to occupy convincing positions around the audience and move naturally between them.

Dynamic Range

The difference between quiet detail and loud impact.

Think about a great picture. Deep blacks retain their shadow detail, while bright highlights remain clear without losing information.

Sound works in much the same way.

A quiet conversation should retain its subtle details, while an explosion or powerful musical passage should arrive with real impact, without strain or distortion.

That contrast is where much of the excitement of cinema lives.

Timbre Matching

A sound should retain its character wherever it comes from.

Imagine a blue snooker ball moving across a television screen. Its colour should remain the same wherever it appears.

The same principle applies to sound.

As a voice, instrument or effect moves between loudspeakers, we want its tonal character to remain consistent.

We also want listeners in different seats to experience a similarly balanced soundtrack, with particular attention to the priority listening positions.

A considered approach

Every room presents different opportunities and constraints. The right design balances Spatial Resolution, Dynamic Range and Timbre Matching to deliver the best overall experience for the room and its intended use.

Achieving the highest possible score in every parameter is not always the most appropriate solution.

04Design

System Design Summary

Design Summary

Voices and effects retain their tonal character as they travel from the screen to the surrounds and overhead, helping the soundtrack feel coherent as it moves around the room. The 115" TV and 9.1.6 layout bring that sound into a dedicated cinema setting, with four subwoofers specified for physical low-frequency impact.

Design Overview

The room is arranged around a 115" TV and a 9.1.6 sound system, with six overhead speakers and four subwoofers supporting effects above and around the audience. At the front, Q6-3 speakers serve left and right, while two C4-1 cabinets reproduce the single centre channel.

Why This Specification

Sound can move above and around the audience rather than remaining at the picture; this movement is supported by the 9.1.6 layout, including six overhead speakers and four subwoofers. Voices and effects also keep a consistent tonal character as they cross between screen, surround and overhead channels, across all 8 assessed seating positions.

The Sound Experience

05

Dynamic Range

Low-frequency effects can carry physical weight in demanding scenes, giving major impacts and musical passages a strong bass foundation. The four-subwoofer system is specified to deliver 115 dBC of low-frequency output at the reference seating position.

06

Spatial Resolution

The front row is positioned to make the most of the 115" TV, with a 57.3° viewing angle for an immersive picture. Around the room, the screen-wall speakers sit within their recommended zones, and the wide and surround speakers are positioned within their recommended limits.

07

Timbre Matching

Voices and effects keep a consistent tonal character as they move from the screen channels to the surrounds and overhead channels. That tonal consistency holds across all 8 assessed seating positions, helping a sound that moves through the cinema retain its character.

09Design

Overall Design

The front row’s view of the 115" TV is paired with sound that can travel around and above the audience, while voices and effects retain their tonal character across the seating positions. Four subwoofers provide the specified low-frequency authority for the weight and impact of demanding film soundtracks.

10Design

Presented Options

Formal cinema seating brings motorised reclining seats and cupholders, while relaxed sofas create an informal arrangement that lets the seating be reconfigured to suit the room. Either option works within the seating positions specified for this design, so the choice comes down to comfort, style and personal preference.
